Orbit & Axle — Issue #1

Week of October 18–24, 2026 · About a 5-minute read

Hi from the Orbit & Axle crew. Welcome to issue #1. Each week we round up what happened across Tesla, SpaceX, xAI, X, Neuralink and The Boring Company, with a link for every item. This week brought two earnings reports, a possible mega-merger and a very well-travelled booster.

The big one: Tesla and SpaceX are formally studying a merger

The boards of Tesla and SpaceX have each set up a special committee of independent directors to look at combining the two companies. Musk has stepped back from the review because he has a conflict of interest. He has said a merger would be "a good idea" and pointed to the AI, chip, robotics and orbital data-centre work the two companies share. Nothing has been agreed. There are no terms yet.

Why it matters: The car company and the rocket company could end up under one roof. That's a long way from settled, so we'll treat it as "under review" until it isn't.

Sources: TechCrunch · The Verge · BBC

Around the empire

Tesla: 486,532 cars delivered, and batteries are the bottleneck

Tesla's third-quarter update, posted Oct. 21, shows 486,532 vehicles delivered, down 2% from a year earlier. Tesla says battery availability "continued to be the limiting factor" on car volumes, even though its passenger-vehicle order backlog is the largest in four years. Capital spending more than doubled from a year earlier, up 110%.

Sources: Tesla Q3 2026 Update (PDF) · CNBC

SpaceX/Starlink: 13.6 million consumer subscribers

In its Q3 results on Oct. 20, SpaceX reported 13.6 million Starlink consumer subscribers as of Sept. 30, up 1.6 million in a quarter. That count leaves out airline, ship and government contracts. More than 3,800 aircraft now have Starlink installed.

Source: SpaceX Q3 2026 results (SEC filing)

SpaceX: booster B1063 flies (and lands) for the 36th time

On Friday, Oct. 23, a Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg at 6:48 p.m. ET carrying 27 Starlink satellites, after three scrubbed attempts. The first stage, B1063, was on its 36th flight and landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You. Back in 2021, the same booster launched NASA's DART asteroid-smashing mission.

Sources: SpaceX mission page · Space.com · Spaceflight Now

The Boring Company: a shipping container, in a tunnel truck

On Oct. 19, The Boring Company showed one of its Prufrock Liner Trucks, which run on Tesla Model 3 batteries and drive units, modified to carry a standard 20-foot shipping container. Its caption: "Fits like a glove!" In a reply, the company said the current version carries 45,000 lb and a 75,000-lb version is planned for January 2027. It hasn't named a route, a customer or a start date.

Sources: Teslarati · Tunnelati

Rumour vs confirmed

🟡 Going around: "Neuralink's Blindsight implant in humans within 6–12 months." A clip of Musk saying this has been shared widely as if it were new. It's from a Y Combinator talk in June 2025, so that window has already closed. Neuralink's own trials page lists the vision study as upcoming, and no human Blindsight implant has been announced. Sources: Neuralink clinical trials page · MobiHealthNews (June 2025) · TokenPost

🟢 Confirmed: The merger committees, Tesla's delivery count, Starlink's 13.6M and B1063's 36th flight all come from company filings, company pages or major outlets (see above).

Canadian corner 🇨🇦

At a press conference in Istanbul on Oct. 23, Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada doesn't want to rely on "one provider that may change their view." He was answering a question about restrictions on Ukraine's access to Starshield, the government version of Starlink. Carney said Canada is "highly, highly reliant on certain systems in certain countries" and is working to diversify.

Sources: CBC News · TeslaNorth
